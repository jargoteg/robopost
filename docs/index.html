Why can't surgical robots swap functions inside your body? This mm-scale soft robot reprograms its magnetization in <1 sec to switch among 5 surgical modes. 6th-DOF rotation unlocks terrain five-DOF bots can't cross. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15610

The genuinely clever bit: rotation about the net magnetic moment (not just position control) gives rolling up to 6.75 mm/s and crawling across unstructured terrain. That sixth DOF is what separates this from prior five-DOF designs that get stuck on any real surface.
Honest caveat: levitation against gravity still needs a buoyant assist. The coil system can't generate enough gradient force alone. So full 3D navigation in tissue is unsolved. How far away do you think we are from a micro-robot that actually navigates inside a living patient?

A Bobcat excavator with a hydraulic hammer now self-selects its own rock targets at 10 Hz, 675 ms latency, on a Jetson AGX Orin. Real hardware. Scaled lab only, but the stack is real. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20748v1

What's clever: one pipeline handles self-filtering, rock instance segmentation, AND kinematically feasible pose generation simultaneously on embedded hardware. That's not trivial at 10 Hz. The ZED X1 stereo pair doing the perception work on a live hydraulic arm is the part I want to see stressed.

A humanoid robot learned crowd navigation from 5 hrs of human walking footage. Zero robot data. Glass-wall avoidance emerges naturally. Runs in 10 diffusion steps, 30 ms on edge hardware. http://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11595528

The clever bit: frozen DINOv3 features catch glass walls that depth sensors miss entirely. The honest bit: authors flag the embodiment gap themselves. Human gait data does not map cleanly onto G1 kinematics. That gap is the real research problem here, not the diffusion model.
Open question for anyone working on humanoid deployment: how much does a 5-hour human dataset actually cover? Crowded malls, yes. Construction sites, narrow corridors, uneven outdoor terrain? This approach needs a stress test outside controlled indoor environments. What breaks first?

1000 for 1000. Every trial. Real robots. RL-100 applies PPO inside diffusion denoising, compresses to one-step control, then runs 7 hrs unattended in a mall. ~90% zero-shot under dynamics shifts. Three arms, eight tasks. https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.aed6267

The clever part is WHERE the RL happens: inside the denoising process of the diffusion policy, not wrapped around it. Clipped PPO keeps it stable across offline then online stages. Consistency distillation gets you to one-step inference without retraining from scratch. Genuinely non-obvious…
Authors are upfront: online RL needs resets, tuning, and real-hardware approvals. That is a real cost most labs cannot absorb easily. So the question is whether the offline RL stages alone get you most of the gains. Code here: https://github.com/lucidrains/RL-100 and video breakdown…

EKF + neural net hybrid estimates contact forces fast enough for a wheel-biped to stay stable mid-manipulation. Clever architecture. Lab results solid. Field terrain is the real exam. http://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11603998

The estimator design is the interesting bit: NN gives you speed, rigid-body dynamics keeps the estimate physically honest. Running both in parallel and fusing via EKF is pragmatic engineering, not just an ablation trick. Worth reading the estimator section closely.
