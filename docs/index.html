Why can't legged robots manipulate and walk at the same time? Usually: two controllers fighting over balance. QLIMB runs one end-to-end policy for both, tested on complex terrain. Clever. I want to see it on a real slope. https://doi.org/10.1109/lra.2026.3712638

Why does syncing humanoid robots make warehouse fleets hard? Each Digit runs its own balance loop. Staying timed to others on top of that is the real problem. Clean studio demo, but the coordination layer is genuinely non-trivial. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vZwYTn3fW1w

The honest caveat: empty floor, no payload, no obstacles. Studio sync and warehouse-floor sync under load are different problems. Balance loops get noisier with weight shifts and surface variation. Would love to see this with a tote in hand.

A humanoid robot learned crowd navigation from 5 hrs of human walking footage. Zero robot data. Glass-wall avoidance emerges naturally. Runs in 10 diffusion steps, 30 ms on edge hardware. http://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11595528

The clever bit: frozen DINOv3 features catch glass walls that depth sensors miss entirely. The honest bit: authors flag the embodiment gap themselves. Human gait data does not map cleanly onto G1 kinematics. That gap is the real research problem here, not the diffusion model.
