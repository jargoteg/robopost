# Content Team — Lessons Learned Brief

## 1. Topics That Over/Under-Performed
**Over:** Locomotion novelty (wave-propulsion SAWbot, 32-legged amoeba, wheel-legged football) and manipulation benchmarks consistently drew reactions. Relatable "why can't robots do X yet" framings also pulled replies. **Under:** Niche sensor/control papers (hysteresis, wrench control, tendon estimation) and drone-fleet logistics attracted near-zero engagement. Medical robotics (breast ultrasound) underperformed relative to its novelty.

## 2. Hook Styles That Worked
Hooks anchoring a *visual surprise* or *human comparison* (legs vs. wheels, hand vs. gripper) outperformed pure stat hooks. Curiosity-gap hooks reliably earned replies even when likes were low.

## 3. Carousel vs. Video
One video post in the dataset (juggling, 0 likes, 1 reply) underperformed comparable carousels. Dataset is too sparse to conclude; **test 3 more videos before drawing conclusions.**

### 3b. Bluesky Thread vs. Single
Threads average ~0.9 replies vs. ~0.4 for singles. Singles edge slightly on likes (1.0 vs 0.8). **Recommended split: 55% thread / 45% single.**

## 4. Three Concrete Recommendations
1. **Lead with human comparison** — hooks framing robot vs. human capability ("why can't a robot do what a diver does") consistently earn replies; apply to next 3 posts.
2. **Drop pure-stat hooks on niche control papers** — number_stat only works when the number is surprising to a non-specialist; gate stat hooks on that test.
3. **Retire low-visual papers earlier** — the rejection pattern confirms figures matter; add a visual-quality filter *before* scripting, not after.
