# Content Team — Lessons Learned Brief

## 1. Topic Performance
**Over-performed:** Humanoid navigation (12L/3R/2Re — clear outlier), wheel-legged robots, multi-robot planning. Human-interest framing ("walks through crowds," "playing football") drove spikes. **Under-performed:** Niche hardware specs (tendons, hysteresis, wrench control), underwater/space arms, and anything without a relatable real-world payoff.

## 2. Hook Styles That Worked
**Bold_claim** produced the top post by a wide margin. Curiosity_gap had modest consistent performance. Number_stat and tension largely underperformed — stats felt abstract, tension framing didn't compel action.

## 3. Carousel vs. Video
Data is too sparse (only 2 videos, both near-zero engagement) to draw conclusions. Test 3–4 more videos before deprioritizing the format.

## 3a. Hook-Style Weighting (evidence-based)
| Hook | Avg likes | Recommended weight |
|---|---|---|
| bold_claim | ~2.4 | **50%** |
| curiosity_gap | ~1.0 | **25%** |
| number_stat | ~0.5 | **15%** |
| tension | ~0.5 | **10%** |

## 3b. Thread vs. Single
Threads average slightly more replies (1.0 vs 0.2) but similar likes. Singles occasionally spike higher. **Recommended split: 50/50** — threads for deeper papers, singles for visual/demo content.

## 4. Three Concrete Recommendations
1. **Lead with humans in the frame** — the humanoid navigation post proves relatable real-world stakes massively outperform pure technical novelty.
2. **Default to bold_claim hooks**; reserve number_stat only when the number is genuinely shocking (>10x improvement).
3. **Reject papers without strong visuals early** — the rejection log confirms this is the single biggest pipeline waste.
