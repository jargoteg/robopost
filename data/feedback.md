# Lessons Learned Brief

## 1. Topic Performance
**Over-performed:** Humanoid/navigation papers with broad appeal ("humanoid learns from human footage" → 16 total engagements). Wheel-legged/physical stunts performed well. Multi-robot planning had modest success.
**Under-performed:** Niche hardware specs (tendon hysteresis, wrench control, bridge-climbing) consistently scored zero. Medical/surgical robotics and underwater arms generated minimal traction despite interesting hooks.

## 2. Hook Styles That Worked
`bold_claim` drove the standout post (11 likes, 3 reposts). `curiosity_gap` produced the most consistent reply generation. `number_stat` and `tension` largely underperformed — stats felt abstract, tension hooks didn't differentiate enough.

## 3. Carousel vs. Video
Data is too sparse (only 2 videos) to conclude. Neither video outperformed carousels meaningfully. **Test needed:** 4–6 more videos before drawing conclusions.

## 3a. Hook-Style Weighting
| Hook | Avg. likes | Recommendation |

| `bold_claim` | ~2.1 | **50% of posts** |
| `curiosity_gap` | ~0.9 | **30%** |

| `tension` | ~0.5 | **10%** |

## 3b. Bluesky Thread vs. Single
Threads average more replies; singles slightly more likes/reposts. Difference is marginal. Recommend **60% thread / 40% single** — threads sustain conversation, valuable for algorithm signaling.

## 4. Three Concrete Recommendations
1. **Prioritize humanoid + locomotion papers** — they hit general tech curiosity beyond robotics insiders.
2. **Lead every post with a `bold_claim` hook** tied to a surprising real-world implication, not a spec.
3. **Retire `number_stat` hooks unless the number is viscerally surprising** (e.g., "5x world record" beats "33.3% better").
