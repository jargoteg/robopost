# Community trends (updated 2026-08-11T07:07Z)

The robotics community is buzzing across several overlapping conversations right now. Humanoid robots are drawing the most energy, split between genuine skepticism ("famously the easiest mode of contemporary robotics" gets eye-rolls) and viral mockery of Russia's collapsing debut robot and Musk's supposed army of "guys in suits." The ethics of AI-in-robotics is heating up, particularly around OpenAI's Pentagon deal and a senior team member's guardrails concerns. A lighter but persistent thread celebrates vintage and mechanical elegance, with the 1821 Oldham coupling and Robert Tinney's 1983 Robotics Age cover art pulling strong engagement. Robot learning research is surfacing too, especially a notable demo where a humanoid learned crowd navigation from five hours of human walking footage with zero robot training data. Swarm robotics is a quieter but growing undercurrent.

A post angle that would land today: a punchy take on the gap between humanoid robot marketing and actual capability, grounded in the crowd-navigation paper as a counterexample of what genuinely impressive robot learning actually looks like.
