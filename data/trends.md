# Community trends (updated 2026-08-12T01:54Z)

The robotics community is buzzing across several fronts right now. Humanoid robots are dominating discourse, but with heavy skepticism — jokes about Russia's collapsing "Aidol" robot, mockery of remote-controlled "autonomous" demos, and broader questioning of whether humanoid form factors make any engineering sense. There's a sharp ongoing debate about AI/robotics as a poverty solution, triggered by a billionaire's comments that's drawing significant pushback. Learning robots are generating quieter but genuine excitement, particularly a humanoid that learned crowd navigation from just five hours of human footage with no robot training data. Classic mechanical engineering is having a moment, with the 1821 Oldham coupling and folded structures going viral for their robotics relevance. Knightscope's pivot back toward human guards is sparking conversation about automation's real-world limits. The Realbotix thread from a robotics researcher is gaining traction too.
