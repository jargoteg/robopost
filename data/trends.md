# Community trends (updated 2026-08-10T07:41Z)

The robotics community is buzzing across several fronts right now. Humanoid robots are drawing heavy skepticism, with recurring jokes about demos being remotely operated, high costs for trivial tasks, and general cynicism about industry hype. The billionaire-savior narrative (specifically the "AI and robotics solve poverty" claim) is generating strong pushback. Structural and mechanical engineering nostalgia is surprisingly popular, with the Oldham coupling and folded-structure posts pulling thousands of engagements, suggesting genuine appetite for foundational knowledge. The Realbotix pivot from sex robots to classroom companions is sparking a serious thread from a robotics researcher worth watching. OpenAI's Pentagon robotics deal and undefined guardrails is a slow-burn concern gaining traction. Swarm robotics appears underrepresented relative to its activity level, mostly niche academic posts.

The post angle that would land today: a sharp, evidence-based take on why the humanoid form factor keeps winning marketing battles despite losing engineering arguments, tying the skepticism threads together with the industrial robotics counterpoint already circulating.
