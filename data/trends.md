# Community trends (updated 2026-08-11T19:02Z)

The robotics community is buzzing across several fronts right now. Humanoid robots dominate conversation, with heavy skepticism about demo authenticity, real-world utility, and the irony of "autonomous" robots being remotely operated by humans. There's sharp cynicism toward billionaire narratives linking robotics to poverty solutions, particularly around Musk. Robot learning and imitation is generating genuine technical interest, especially a notable demo of a humanoid learning crowd navigation from just 5 hours of human walking footage with no robot training data. The Realbotix pivot from sex robots to companion robots is sparking a substantive thread from a researcher worth following. Knightscope's security robot failures and the pivot back to human guards is drawing engagement around the limits of automation. Swarm robotics is a quieter but growing undercurrent.

A post angle that would land today: contrast the humanoid hype cycle with the crowd-navigation paper's genuinely elegant result, framing it as "this is what real robot learning progress actually looks like."
